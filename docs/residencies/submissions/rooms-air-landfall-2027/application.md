## Artist Bio

Adam Simms is a media artist from Ktaqmkuk (Newfoundland), based in Tiohtià:ke (Montreal). Through photography, video, sculpture, and microelectronics, he traces how fragments of place, language, and culture persist across distance and time.

For more than a decade, his practice has centred on Pinchard's Island, the treeless outport his grandmother was resettled from in the 1960s. Through his family's history, he asks which aspects of intangible culture are portable and which are rooted in place.

_Cloudberry_, _Cabin_, and _Driftwood_ used solar cameras, real-time data, and sensors to make distance material, transmitting landscape and vernacular from remote sites into other rooms and cities. His work has appeared in _Riddle Fence_ and _Ciel variable_, and been exhibited at the Leonard & Bina Ellen Art Gallery and Occurrence. He holds a BFA in Design and an MFA in Studio Arts from Concordia University, where he has also taught as an Artist-in-Residence.

## Statement of interest (500 to 700 words)

**THE BERRY**

For three weeks at Kent Cottage, only the Brigus sun will grow a bakeapple. The sculpture will build layer by layer on a 3D printer powered by my portable solar panel and battery. A sunny day adds more layers and a day of fog barely any, so the finished berry will carry a record of its weather. It should reach about sixteen inches tall by the end of the residency. Once it is complete, a solar-powered light inside will keep the bakeapple responsive to the sun that shaped it.

My grandmother Doris picked the original bakeapple on Pinchard's Island. Although she and her family left the island in the 1960s, they still return to pick bakeapples in the summer. Resettlement took the houses but not the tradition of picking berries, which lives on in whoever still knows which bog, when to go, and how to make the jam. As the last generation of Pinchard's Islanders fades, I am asking the berry to hold what is left of the island, without pretending to preserve what is gone. Bakeapples are tiny, but what they carry is not, and this one is grown to that scale.

I scanned the berry my grandmother gave me from more than a thousand photographs into a 3D file, an attempt to preserve that one berry and the days we spent picking together on the island. A generic bakeapple would speak for everyone, but this one speaks only for where it was picked.

**RESIDENCY PLAN**

I will have a car for the full three weeks, and I will bring the printer, portable solar panel, and battery with me. The printer sits on a desk inside, and the panel needs only an open spot on the ground in direct sun. The berry draws no power from the cottage, and nothing is fixed to the building or grounds. After the residency, the berry travels with me to be exhibited elsewhere. While the printer runs, I will program an LED light to make the berry glow once the print is finished. I will experiment with how the light behaves, whether it holds steady or pulses, whether it comes on every night or only in fog, and how bright it should be on a cliff over Brigus Bay.

**WHAT I BRING TO THE PROGRAM**

I come to Landfall as a media artist who builds solar-powered work. Like the cottage, this project runs only on the Brigus sun, and it takes that limit as its material, so the berry becomes a way to see the summer's light. I will log each day's weather, energy, and printing so the berry's form can be read against the conditions that made it. I will leave that log with the Trust so future residents can learn to work with what the sun allows.

**WHAT THE RESIDENCY BRINGS TO MY PRACTICE**

For more than a decade I have made work about Pinchard's Island from a distance. _Cloudberry_, a solar camera on our family cabin, streamed the island to me in Tiohtià:ke (Montreal) until too little winter sun forced it to sleep, and _Driftwood_, a log hung in a gallery, rose and fell with live data from an ocean buoy a thousand kilometres away. _Light House_ traced our cabin in solar-powered light on the Newfoundland shore. Across that work, distance was the material, and each work lasted only as long as its signal, sun, or site.

Kent Cottage gets its electricity only from the sun, and for three weeks so will the berry and I. The limit my earlier works struggled against becomes the condition of making. At Landfall I will explore whether the sun can grow a berry and keep it alive.

## Public event

In consultation with the Landfall Trust.

Toward the end of the residency, I will host an artist talk and a mug up with tea, snacks, and bakeapple jam. I will show Bakeapple for the first time, a sculpture 3D-printed over three weeks using only the Brigus sun, and present the earlier works that led to it. At dusk, the light inside the sculpture will come on, powered by the day's sun.
